# Evidence handling and scientific standards

This laboratory uses synthetic forensic specimens. Treat the generated files as your case dataset, while recognizing that a sample log, specimen, or packet capture may not provide exhaustive visibility.

1. Validate the provided SHA-256 custody manifest before beginning; report discrepancies.
2. Cite exact artifacts, record identifiers, UTC timestamps and byte offsets where relevant.
3. Distinguish observation, inference, alternative hypothesis and unresolved information.
4. Do not silently merge events across clocks with unknown calibration errors.
5. Do not assume two sources are independent merely because their filenames differ.
6. Do not mistake a signature, approval entry, ticket, service label or local assertion for verified execution.
7. State `NOT ESTABLISHED` if the proposition exceeds the collected evidence; name a discriminating test.
8. End with practical containment, independent validation and recovery conditions.

A demonstrably limited finding with explicit collection requirements is more defensible than an unsupported high-confidence allegation.
